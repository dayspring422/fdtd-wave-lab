The update equations

In 2D with E pointing out of the screen (TMz mode), Maxwell's curl equations reduce to three coupled equations:

∂Ez/∂t =  (1/ε) (∂Hy/∂x − ∂Hx/∂y)
∂Hx/∂t = −(1/μ)  ∂Ez/∂y
∂Hy/∂t =  (1/μ)  ∂Ez/∂x

The Yee grid staggers E and H by half a cell in space and half a step in time, so each update uses centered differences:

Hx[i,j] ← Hx[i,j] − S (Ez[i,j+1] − Ez[i,j])
Hy[i,j] ← Hy[i,j] + S (Ez[i+1,j] − Ez[i,j])
Ez[i,j] ← Ez[i,j] + (S/εr) [ (Hy[i,j] − Hy[i−1,j])
                           − (Hx[i,j] − Hx[i,j−1]) ]
S = cΔt/Δx = 0.5   (2D stability needs S ≤ 1/√2)

Fields are normalized so E and H share units. A conductor forces Ez = 0. Glass has εr = n², which slows the wave to c/n. The edges are wrapped in a 20-cell absorbing layer with graded, impedance-matched loss, so waves leave the screen instead of bouncing back.
